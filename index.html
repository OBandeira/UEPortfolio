About

I’m Olinda Bandeira, a graduate student in Urban Informatics at Northeastern University. Before moving into the world of urban data, I spent more than a decade working in banking, with a focus on auditing, risk, compliance, and internal controls.

My interest in Urban Informatics grew from a simple question: how can we use data not only to understand problems, but also to make better decisions? Through my studies, I’ve been building skills in data analysis, visualization, GIS, and statistical methods, applying them to real-world urban and community issues.

I enjoy turning complex data into clear, meaningful insights and exploring how those insights can support better policies, programs, and communities.

  • Data analysis
  • Data visualization
  • GIS
  • Statistical methods
  • Audit & internal controls
  • Risk & compliance

Community-Inspired Food Safety Metric

Data Analyst & Urban Researcher

Developed a Complaint-to-Violation Ratio (CVR) metric to examine food safety disparities along Boston's Blue Hill Avenue corridor. By linking health inspection data (REI), 311 complaint reports, and neighborhood demographics (ACS), the project uncovers reporting gaps influenced by income, language barriers, and community knowledge—revealing how civic participation and public safety interact across neighborhoods.

Coffee Shop Competitive Mapping

Geospatial Data Analyst

Analyzed Dunkin' Donuts and Starbucks distribution across eastern Massachusetts using Google Places API and demographic data from Social Explorer. Findings reveal Dunkin's "be everywhere" strategy dominates (80% of outlets) across all income levels, while Starbucks targets premium markets (median income ≈$90K). Competitive hotspots emerge in mid-income counties ($75K–$85K), informing strategic growth opportunities for both brands.

Flood Risk & Socioeconomic Vulnerability

GIS Analyst & Policy Researcher

GIS analysis examining the intersection of flood risk and income inequality in Boston. The research maps climate vulnerabilities across neighborhoods, revealing disproportionate exposure in low-income communities lacking recovery resources. Policy recommendations prioritize resilience infrastructure, equitable resource allocation, and community engagement to support climate adaptation in the most vulnerable areas.
